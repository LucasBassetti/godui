While the pointer is down on a thumb, `active:scale-[1.15]` makes it grow,
on the bouncy spring so it overshoots slightly and settles. It's the
inverse of a button press: a button sinks under your finger, a slider thumb
lifts toward it, so you can see what you're holding.

shadcn transitions the thumb's `color` and `box-shadow`, which means the
hover ring tweens in by repainting. GodUI snaps the ring and animates only
`scale`.

</LearnChapter>

<LearnChapter
  label="Dragging isn't animation"
  scene={<SpringCurveScene label="Dragging isn't animation" easing="snappy" />}
  code={`// Radix positions the thumb from the value, every pointermove:
<span style={{ left: \`calc(\${percent}% + \${offset}px)\` }}>
  <SliderPrimitive.Thumb … />
</span>`}
>

The thumb and the range follow the pointer exactly. Radix sets their
position from the current value on every `pointermove`, and nothing eases
between frames. That's on purpose: an eased drag lags behind your finger and
feels broken.

Each move writes a new `left`, so dragging does run layout, but only as often
as the pointer reports, and only while you're dragging. No animation runs,
so there's no layout tween to move onto the compositor. GodUI's motion
budget goes to the moment you grab and release the thumb.

</LearnChapter>

## Why GPU-only

The only animation is the thumb's `scale`. The drag is direct manipulation and
doesn't animate at all.

## Reduced motion

`motion-reduce:transition-none` makes the thumb grow instantly when held.
