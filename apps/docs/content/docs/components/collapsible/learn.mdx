When a collapsible opens, its panel needs room. Animating `height` would
lay out the page on every frame, so the height changes in one step. The
panel slides in from slightly above, and everything that got pushed down
plays a `translate` from its old position (FLIP).

As in [Accordion](/docs/components/accordion/learn), a tiny signal component
inside the panel bumps a counter when the panel mounts and when it unmounts,
which is the exact commit where the height changes. On close, the panel fades
out first, then collapses.

useFlipGroup(parentRef, version, { selector: ":scope > *" });`}

An accordion's items are its own children, so it FLIPs inside itself. A
collapsible usually stands alone, and the things that move when it opens are
whatever comes after it: the next card, a paragraph, a footer. Those are
siblings, not children.

So the FLIP group is the Collapsible root's parent, and the candidates are
that parent's direct children. Earlier siblings and the Collapsible itself
don't move, so only the elements after it animate. Anything further up the
tree moves in one step.

## Why GPU-only

The panel animates `opacity` and `translate`; the siblings animate `translate`.
No `height` is ever animated.

## Reduced motion

`useFlipGroup` skips the slide, so siblings jump to their new position, and
the panel fades for 150ms.
